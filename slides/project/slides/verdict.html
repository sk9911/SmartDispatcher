AI vs physics · stress test · caveats

Who wins, where it breaks, what to watch

Who's who

AI: GraphCast (DeepMind), Pangu-Weather (Huawei)

Physics: IFS HRES (ECMWF)

Hybrid: NeuralGCM. Its 25-member ensemble plan, 121 kg at 120 h, beats every single forecast.

Baseline: persistence, today's weather held fixed

Record-jet night, 9 Feb 2020

Fastest eastbound crossing in the set: 281 min vs 319 min typical.

12–48 h: IFS HRES matched the hindsight plan (0 kg).

120 h: GraphCast 193 kg, Pangu 325 kg, IFS HRES 492 kg.

Persistence: up to 2.8 t.

Read before quoting

The 1.5° grid blunts HRES's 9 km detail and the jet core.

AI sources are trained on ERA5 and scored against it, which may favour them.

One route, one aircraft, two winter months: spread, not significance.

For dispatch, 0–3 days out, AI and physics forecasts are equal. The AI edge appears at 5 days, and part of it may be a scoring effect.

Source: reports/report.html and data/results/*.parquet. The ERA5 scoring caveat is our reading, not a claim in the report.

GraphCast and Pangu-Weather are pure machine-learning models trained on ERA5. IFS HRES is ECMWF's physics model. NeuralGCM is a hybrid, tested in a reduced add-on. On the 9 February 2020 record jet night, the forecasts held up to 72 hours; at 5 days IFS HRES lost 492 kg on that one flight. Caveats: everything runs on a coarse 1.5 degree grid, the AI models were trained on the same ERA5 data used as the truth, and the sample is one route, one aircraft and two months.
